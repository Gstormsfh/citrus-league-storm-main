How this works
Every number on this page is measured, and the percentiles are regular‑season only. The game-log table also holds 82 playoff games, and a league baseline built without excluding them is quietly wrong. Faceoffs and shot attempts are here for the first time: 1.37 million draws and 1.38 million attempts across nine seasons, extracted from play‑by‑play we already held and verified against the invariant that league faceoff wins must equal losses exactly, 50.000% in all nine, independently. One thing is still missing: deployment and strength splits, because the shift feed stopped ingesting on 10 January and 738 games have never been backfilled. That needs a job restarted, not a query, and we know the date.

Why he leaves Matthews on the board: Stormy takes the two best forwards available, a checking centre and a pair that blocks, then hands you the best goalscorer in the league and dares you to build around him. How this works
Why typing beats multiple choice: four names on screen turns a memory test into a one-in-four guess, and a fan who gets it that way has not proved anything to himself. Naming him cold is the game, so the four-name hint still exists and costs fifteen points to open. Game 05 · live

Heat Check

Six lines, each set at that Leaf’s own season rate. Over or under.

6 linesHis own rateSettles at the buzzer
0 of 6
How this works
Why the line is his own average: a house line is a number a fan has to trust. A line drawn from the player's own season is one they can check, argue with, and beat by knowing something the average does not: that he is on the top power play tonight, or coming off an injury, or matched against a fourth pair. That is the skill this game is testing.
Game 06 · live

Rank 'Em

4 LeafsOne statSettles at the buzzer

How this works
Why four and not ten: ordering ten men is a memory exercise and nobody finishes it on a concourse. Four is the number a fan can hold in their head between whistles, and because all four are drawn from the same end of the season leaderboard the gaps between them are small enough that form, matchup and who is on the top pair actually decide it. The season rate is printed on every row, so the fan who ignores it and backs the man he watched last week is making a real call rather than a blind one.

How this works
Why the pin and not the outcome: every fan in the building already shouts about who is going to score. Almost nobody has to say where from, and that is the read that separates someone who watches the team from someone who reads the box score. Every goal in this game carries real coordinates out of 1,029,285 play‑by‑play rows, so the gap between your pin and the spot he actually scored from is a measurement in feet, not a number we made up. Twenty‑five points for picking a man who scores at all, up to a hundred more for how close the pin was, running out at forty‑five feet. Toronto beat Anaheim six‑four off six different sticks, so three picks is a real call and the safe three are not the accurate three.
