WHY V1 WAS WRONG, SO IT DOES NOT HAPPEN AGAIN

The first brief asked for "flat vector, thick uniform dark navy outline on
every shape, completely flat fills, no gradients, no shading, no texture, no
highlights." That was written to match the PAGE — Toronto navy, one accent,
flat UI — and it is the wrong target. The characters are not UI.

Put the four Citrus mascots next to what came back and the gap is the whole
story. Stormy, Pineapple, Kiwi and Lemon are **3D renders**: low-poly faceted
surfaces, soft matte shading, a light source up and to the left, gentle
ambient occlusion in the creases. **Not one of them has an outline.** Their
palette is muted and slightly earthy — sage green, dusty gold, worn navy — not
the bright page tokens. Their kit is real: tan wooden sticks, dark textured
gloves, ribbed pads, proper skates with blades. Their faces have pupils,
highlights, eyebrows, attitude. Kiwi wears glasses. Lemon looks annoyed.

Carlton v1 is a flat sticker with a hard navy keyline and a blank smile. He
does not belong to that family and no amount of palette matching fixes it.

The four attached images are the house style. Study them closely and match
them exactly: stylised 3D character renders with low-poly faceted surfaces,
soft matte shading, a soft key light from the upper left, gentle ambient
occlusion, and a slightly gritty hand-finished texture. They have NO
outlines, NO keylines and NO flat vector fills of any kind — every form reads
through shading and silhouette alone. Their colours are muted and earthy, not
bright or saturated.

Create a new character for that same family: CARLTON, a polar bear ice hockey
mascot. He must look like he was rendered in the same session as the other
four, by the same hand, in the same engine.

The bear: soft off-white to pale grey fur with a warm cream muzzle and belly,
faceted low-poly surfacing like the kiwi's skin, small rounded ears, a dark
matte nose, and an expressive friendly face with real pupils, catchlights and
eyebrows. Give him a character — warm and a little goofy, the way Lemon is a
little annoyed. Chunky and rounded, a mascot build, not an athlete's.

His kit, all of it visible and clearly separate from his body: a worn navy
hockey sweater with a white shoulder yoke and white hem stripes, navy hockey
pants, shoulder pads sitting proud on top of the sweater, dark textured
gloves, ribbed leg pads down both shins, and proper skates with visible steel
blades. He holds a tan wooden hockey stick, blade resting on the ice, and a
black puck sits beside the blade. Render the kit with the same materials the
other four have — the pineapple's pads, the kiwi's skates, the lemon's stick.

On his chest, where a team crest would sit: a small orange citrus slice, in
the same understated way the pineapple carries his orange dot. It is a detail,
not a billboard.

POSE, AND THIS PART IS NOT STYLE, IT IS MECHANICS: standing square to camera,
upright, both skates flat on the ice and apart, arms down and slightly away
from the body, like the pineapple goalie's stance. Every piece of kit must be
fully visible and not overlapping another piece — the shoulder pads clear of
the head, both gloves clear of the body, both leg pads clear of each other,
both skates clear, the stick clear of the legs, the puck clear of the stick.
Do not use a dynamic or three-quarter pose, however much better it would look.
The application cuts this figure into six separate pieces and lights them one
at a time.

Transparent background. No ground, no shadow, no scenery, no backdrop.
Full body, whole figure in frame with a little air around it. Portrait, 4:5.

NO TEXT, NO LETTERING, NO NUMBERS, NO LOGOS and NO TEAM MARKS of any kind
other than the small orange citrus slice on his chest. No maple leaf.

ONE THING WORTH KNOWING

The pose constraint and the house style pull against each other. The other
four are posed dynamically because nothing cuts them up. Carlton has to stand
still because the paper doll takes him apart. If the square-on stance comes
back stiff next to the others, say so and we can talk about whether the
mechanic or the pose gives — but do not solve it by posing him, because that
breaks the game rather than the picture.
