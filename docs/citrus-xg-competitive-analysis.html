Mathematical & Statistical Methods

Where Citrus uses quantitative finance techniques that no fantasy platform implements
Quant-Grade
Student-t Copula
What: Models joint distribution of player outcomes with tail dependence.

Why it matters: Gaussian copula has zero tail dependence — it treats extreme co-occurrence as impossible. Student-t with ν=5 gives ~18% probability of extreme co-movement.

Real-world impact: Two linemates both having 5-point games is rare but real. Our model captures this; others can’t.
Finance-Grade
Bayesian Conjugate Priors
What: Beta-Binomial model for finishing talent posterior.

Why it matters: A player with 3 goals on 10 shots (30% sh%) isn’t necessarily elite — small sample. Our Beta(α+goals, β+shots−goals) posterior shrinks toward the league mean (10%) with proper uncertainty.

Others: Use raw stats or season-long averages with no uncertainty.
Bayesian
Cholesky Decomposition
What: Factorizes the 8×8 stat correlation matrix (L × LT) for efficient correlated sampling.

Why it matters: Goals and SOG are correlated (ρ=0.55). PPP and Goals (ρ=0.42). Sampling independently would understate variance of composite fantasy scores.

Others: Assume stat independence (massive error).
Linear Algebra
Antithetic Variates
What: For each uniform sample U, generate mirror 1−U. Doubles effective sample count.

Why it matters: Reduces CI width on win probability by ~30% for free. Same as using 20K samples but with 10K computation cost.

Others: N/A (don’t do MC simulation at all).
Variance Reduction
Brier Score Calibration
What: BS = mean((p − outcome)²). Tracks prediction quality per league per week with bucketed calibration curves.

Why it matters: A model that says "70% win chance" should win ~70% of the time. Without calibration tracking, you’re flying blind.

Others: No fantasy platform tracks projection accuracy.
Calibration
Kendall’s τ → Pearson Conversion
What: Empirical rank correlation from game-by-game fantasy co-movement, converted via ρ = sin(π/2 × τ).

Why it matters: Captures emergent correlations that structural data (shifts/assists) might miss. More robust to outliers than Pearson directly.

Others: No player correlation modeling at all.
