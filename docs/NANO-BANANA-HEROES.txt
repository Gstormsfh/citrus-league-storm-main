CITRUS — ONE RENDER PER PAGE
Ten images. Each one replaces a plate AND a cut-out.

WHY

The build has been compositing two separately-generated images: an empty
arena plate, and a character knocked out of a studio render. They were never
lit together. Different key light, different camera height, different
horizon, different ground plane. A drop shadow under the skates is a fake
relationship — it will always read as pasted, because it is pasted.

The fix is not a better cut-out. It is one image where the character is
already standing in the room, lit by that room's lights, casting that room's
shadow. Ten of those and the layering problem stops existing.

The build already prefers these. Drop one in and it supersedes both layers
for that page; the pages without one keep the current two-layer version, so
you can deliver these one at a time.


================================================================
THE METHOD — reference, don't regenerate
================================================================

Feed the existing -tor render as the SOURCE IMAGE every time. This is the
same discipline that kept the sweaters on-model through phases 1 and 2: the
model is placing a character it can see, not inventing one from a
description. Describe him in the prompt anyway — belt and braces — but the
reference image is what keeps him the same character across ten scenes.

--- PRESERVATION BLOCK, paste on every one ---

Use the character in the reference image EXACTLY as he is: same face, same
eye shape and colour, same expression, same body proportions, same navy and
white hockey sweater with the same number, same badge, same equipment. Do
not redesign him, do not restyle him, do not change his colours. Same chunky
low-poly 3D style throughout.

--- SCENE BLOCK, paste on every one ---

Place him INTO the scene described below as ONE single continuous render —
not a cut-out on a background. He stands on the scene's floor, lit by the
scene's own lights, casting a real contact shadow onto that floor, with the
scene's colour bouncing onto him. Same lens and camera height as the room.

Wide cinematic 16:9, 1920x1080. Deep navy blue (#00205B, #041030) dominant.
Matte materials, soft bevels, soft studio-quality light.

COMPOSITION IS NOT OPTIONAL: the headline sits on the left, so the LEFT 55%
of the frame stays dark, low contrast and empty of any focal object. The
character stands in the RIGHT third. All scene detail lives in the right 45%.

NO OTHER PEOPLE OR CREATURES. ABSOLUTELY NO TEXT, NO LETTERS, NO NUMBERS, NO
SIGNAGE, NO LOGOS anywhere in the frame — if a surface would normally carry
writing, leave it blank.

--- end blocks ---

================================================================
HOW TO TELL IT WORKED
================================================================

Only one test matters, and it is not "does the character look right."

  Cover the character with your thumb. Does the light on the floor where he
  was standing still make sense? Is there a shadow, and does it fall the
  same way as every other shadow in the room?

If yes, it is one render. If the floor under him is clean and unlit, the
model pasted him in and you have the same problem in a new file.

Two more, both fatal:
  - his shadow falls a different direction from the room's other shadows
  - he is lit from a side the room has no light on

Text appearing anywhere is still the failure mode from phase 1. Say the
no-text line twice if you have to.


================================================================
DROP THEM IN
================================================================

    (into apps/web/public/mascots/, then)
    python3 bake_art.py && python3 build.py

The build takes `hero-*.webp` over the plate-plus-cut-out pair for that page
automatically, hides the character layer, and drops the fake contact shadow
with it. Pages without one keep what they have now, so deliver these one at
a time and watch them replace themselves.
