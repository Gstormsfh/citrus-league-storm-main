Part 5 · The Math Other Platforms Don’t Have
Σ

Six techniques borrowed from quant finance — in plain English

Why these specific tools, and what they buy a fantasy user
Quant-Grade
Student-t Copula (ν=5)
What: Models joint player outcomes with tail dependence.

Why: A Gaussian copula treats “both linemates exploded tonight” as essentially impossible. Student-t with ν=5 gives ~18% probability of co-extreme outcomes — closer to reality.

What you see: When you start one Edmonton forward, the matchup win-probability properly accounts for the other one too.
Tail-Aware
Bayesian Conjugate Priors
What: Beta(α+goals, β+shots−goals) posterior over a player’s true finishing talent, with NHL-mean prior.

Why: A callup with 3 goals on 10 shots isn’t a 30% shooter. Our model shrinks to the league mean with proper uncertainty until the sample stabilizes.

What you see: No more roster-burned panic chasing fluky hot streaks.
Bayesian
Cholesky Decomposition
What: Factorizes the 8×8 stat-correlation matrix (L × LT) for efficient correlated sampling.

Why: Goals ↔ SOG correlate at ρ=0.55. PPP ↔ Goals at ρ=0.42. Sampling them independently massively understates fantasy-point variance.

What you see: Honest confidence intervals on your weekly score.
Linear Algebra
Antithetic Variates
What: For every uniform sample U, also use 1−U. Same compute, ~2× effective samples.

Why: Cuts CI width on win probability by ~30% with zero added cost.

What you see: A win-probability number that doesn’t wobble between refreshes.
Variance Reduction
Brier Calibration
What: BS = mean((p − outcome)2). Tracks: did 70%-probability events actually happen 70% of the time?

Why: Without calibration tracking, “73% win” is just a vibe. With it, it’s a number you can take to the bank.

What you see: A calibration badge on every matchup — we show our work.
Calibration
Kendall’s τ → Pearson
What: Empirical rank correlation from game-by-game fantasy co-movement, converted via ρ = sin(π/2 × τ).

Why: Catches emergent correlations (goalie-defense synergies, system-driven duos) that structural shift data alone would miss. Robust to outliers.

What you see: Correlations that reflect how your team actually plays, not just who shares ice time.

Part 7 · Why This Can’t Be Copied In Six Months
∞

The infrastructure stack that compounds

Each layer takes time to build, and they only work when stacked — every layer is a wall
Compounding
Layer 1
Raw play-by-play ingestion
863K shots across 7 NHL seasons stored shot-by-shot with coordinates, pass context, shifts, and zone entries. 100-IP proxy rotation, circuit breakers, and a 24/7 scraping service. Competitors use pre-aggregated NHL API box scores — they can’t compute pass-context xG even if they wanted to.
~18 moTo Replicate
Layer 2
31-feature xG model
10-feature pass quality module (cross-ice, royal road, one-timer, slot-to-slot, goalie disruption). 6-feature rebound/sequence module. Requires Layer 1 to exist. Training, validation, and calibration takes months of iteration to push AUC-ROC past 0.81.
~9 moTo Replicate
Layer 3
Data-mined correlations
Shift-overlap mining from player_shifts_official. Assist-chain mining from raw_shots. Kendall’s τ co-movement from game logs. Three independent correlation sources — each requires Layer 1 raw data plus a separate query pipeline.
~6 moTo Replicate
Layer 4
Monte Carlo engine
5-class architecture (PlayerDistribution, CorrelationEngine, MatchupSimulator, SeasonSimulator, BrierTracker). Student-t copula with tail dependence. Cholesky-factored 8×8 stat-correlation. Antithetic variates + stratified sampling. Vectorized fantasy scoring. Production-grade quant code, not a research notebook.
~6 moTo Replicate
Layer 5
Calibration + audit
Per-league per-week Brier score tracking. Bucketed calibration curves. Transaction ledger for every roster move. 45+ RLS policies, SECURITY DEFINER hardening, zero cross-league leakage. This is the layer that turns “we have a model” into “we have a trustworthy product”.
~4 moTo Replicate
Layer 6
Real-time draft engine
Persistent stateful WebSocket draft engine with sub-300ms pick fanout, sub-1s autopick latency. In-memory state per draft, event-log replay on restart. Competitive with Yahoo & ESPN on the live draft experience — plus all of the above informing every pick.
~6 moTo Replicate
The layers don’t work without each other
You can’t build Layer 3 without Layer 1. You can’t ship Layer 4 to users without Layer 5. That’s ~4 years of work compressed into 18 months — and the timer doesn’t start until somebody decides hockey is worth the investment. Yahoo and ESPN have spent two decades not making that decision.
