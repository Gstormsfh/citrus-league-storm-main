# Development-only FPAR foundation adapter

`packages/shared/src/utils/fparFoundation.ts` is an additive, non-serving adapter. It is deliberately not in the shared export barrel. It leaves the frozen Python one-position replacement contract and the actual scoring calculator unchanged. It generates no forecasts, reads no database, and grants no publication authority.

## Input and verification boundary

`calculateFparFoundation(input, { now, verifyEvidence })` is asynchronous because it computes SHA-256 bindings with Web Crypto. Inputs require a league and team identity, season, explicit inclusive horizon, aware as-of timestamp, exact points-scoring settings, complete expected player inventory, owned roster, scoring slots, supplied lineup, optional explicit add/drop, and foundation/forecast/league/eligibility receipt references. Every player's scope must match exactly.

Each player supplies authoritative atomic position eligibility (`C`, `LW`, `RW`, `D`, or `G`), skater/goalie kind, explicit `owned`/`available`/`unavailable` ownership and `active`/`ir` state. There is no permissive position parser, primary-position fallback, assumption that absent IR means active, or inference of free-agent availability. A flexible slot declares the actual positions it accepts; unresolved generic eligibility must be resolved upstream, not guessed here.

Every relevant scoring statistic must be a finite horizon-total physical forecast, including statistics whose configured weight is zero. Skaters require goals, assists, power-play points, short-handed points, shots on goal, blocks, hits, penalty minutes, and plus/minus. Goalies require wins, shutouts, saves, and goals against. Expected values may be fractional. Counts are nonnegative; plus/minus may be signed. Power-play plus short-handed points cannot exceed goals plus assists. This adapter requires goals within the forecast on-goal population, so goals cannot exceed shots on goal; a different population needs explicit upstream reconciliation. Shutouts are not constrained by wins.

Units are explicit: `expected_count_over_horizon`, `expected_penalty_minutes_over_horizon`, or `expected_goal_differential_over_horizon`. Per-game rates, minutes interpreted as seconds, aliases, nulls, nonfinite numbers, missing fields and unknown forecast status are rejected, never filled with zero. Scoring settings must contain every supported weight, including explicit plus/minus; there is no league-default fallback. After validation and verification, scoring calls the existing `ScoringCalculator.calculatePoints` directly.

The required verifier receives exact SHA-256 bindings for the frozen input, scope, scoring, forecasts, eligibility/availability, and roster, plus receipt references. It must return `status: verified` and the identical binding. A Boolean approval, absent verifier, detached scoring/forecast scope, or unavailable foundation receipt cannot proceed. The integrator must actually check those bindings against trusted complete physical-forecast, league, eligibility and foundation evidence. An unconditional echo verifier is useful only in explicitly synthetic tests; hashes and status strings do not authenticate a source or establish forecast quality. Real and synthetic evidence stay labeled, and every result remains `publishable: false`. Caller inputs are copied before awaiting the verifier.

## Two deliberately different quantities

The replacement lineup maximizes total projected points from the complete declared **available, active** pool, with each player assigned at most once and every supplied slot filled compatibly. A bounded rectangular assignment algorithm handles competing multi-position players jointly. Independently taking the best center and best left wing could incorrectly reuse the same player; this adapter cannot do that. An infeasible pool withholds the result rather than inventing a zero replacement.

For the caller's supplied feasible lineup, each slot's player FPAR is its projected points minus that slot's points in the single fixed replacement allocation. These values add to supplied-lineup points minus replacement-lineup points. This is a **role- and snapshot-specific attribution**, not an intrinsic player scalar transferable between positions, leagues or horizons. Slots and players are deterministically ordered for ties; equally optimal replacement allocations can have different individual attributions even when their total is identical. Negative FPAR and genuinely zero points are preserved.

Optional roster-move opportunity value instead compares the best feasible owned-roster lineup before and after the explicit add/drop, permitting positional reassignment and use of owned bench players. It is labeled non-additive. It is not the sum of individual FPAR changes, and multiple move values cannot be added independently. The reference replacement lineup remains fixed to the original snapshot.

## Limits and integration blockers

Operational bounds are 1,000 players, 32 scoring slots and 64 owned players. These are resource limits, not league defaults or quality thresholds. Evidence errors throw `FPAR unavailable`; callers must retain that unavailable state, not substitute zero.

This first adapter supports a static common-horizon additive points objective only. It does not model daily lineup scheduling, future eligibility/IR changes, games-start caps, waiver priority or claims, transaction limits, salaries, roster-lock timing, category/rotisserie utility, forecast uncertainty, or interactions between simultaneous moves. Those require explicitly versioned constraints before a broader optimization claim. An unsupported format or infeasible allocation is rejected.

There is no verified live physical-forecast provider or foundation verifier wired to this adapter, and no production consumer activation. The tests use synthetic forecasts and synthetic verifier receipts; their computed values are not trustworthy live FPAR evidence. Practical integration requires exact real league/team/ownership/eligibility snapshots, full-horizon calibrated physical-stat forecasts with their units and coverage, a real upstream verification implementation, a product decision on the horizon/slot constraints, and separate consumer/rollout validation. Existing frozen contracts, forecasts and serving paths are preserved, not silently replaced.
